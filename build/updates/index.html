v0.408 — Auto-interact, reactions and haste (still beta)

For players

  • Your character fights back. When an enemy hits you and you're standing still with no other target (or it is your target), your character swings back on its own. It never switches a target you picked, never pulls a healer off an ally, and never turns you around while you run. Turn it off in Options: React Automatically.
  • Clicking an enemy you're standing on works. Up close your own character could be "in front" of the enemy, so a right-click hit you and stopped your attack. A click now picks the enemy.

For campaign makers

  • Auto-attack is now auto-interact (it repeats any interaction: a swing, a heal, tending a crop). Renamed with no aliases while we're in beta: auto_attack → auto_interact, auto_attack_range → auto_interact_range, auto_attack_interval → auto_interact_interval, start_auto_attack → start_auto_interact, stop_auto_attack_self → stop_auto_interact_self. Rename these in your campaign.
  • reaction on an Actor runs whenever another actor's action lands on it (the counterpart of on_interact), filtered by its own if.
  • auto_interact_speed: a dice percentage, read before each repeat. Point it at a stat for haste and slows.
  • start_auto_interact takes an optional chase: false; new built-in values auto_interacting, moving and @is_target.
  • The Auto-attack recipe shows all of it: right-click to attack, fight back, haste.

v0.407 — Character page and HUD fixes (still beta)

A small update on top of the 0.405 release candidate.

For players

  • One target plate, always. An old target's plate could stay at the top of the screen beside the new one after you changed form or map, or talked to someone. Now there is only ever one.
  • Character page items are icons. Pick a slot and what fits it shows as a grid of icons. Rest the mouse on an item (or on a slot) to see its name and what it does. The item you're wearing has a gold border.
  • New focus corner icon. The corners show a small figure in crosshairs instead of a pin.

For campaign makers

  • paired slots. A Slot with paired: true says that worn[i] goes with choices[i], so the Character page can mark the worn item. Both lists must be the same length. See the Slot reference and the Character page recipe. Campaigns that use it need 0.407 or newer.

v0.405 — Release candidate (still beta)

Isometry 0.405 is a release candidate. It is still a beta, but everything below is finished, tested (1,990 automated tests, all passing) and played in a full campaign, Aurelia. This is everything since 0.401.

For players

  • Quests page (J). Every quest you've started, what to do now, and the steps you've done. Tick Show completed to see finished ones.
  • Character page (U). What you wear in each slot and your key stats. Pick a slot to swap gear.
  • Talent trees (P). Spend points from the bottom row up; each talent shows what it does.
  • Auto-attack. Right-click an enemy and your character keeps swinging until it dies, you change target or you walk away. Casting a skill in between doesn't reset your swing.
  • Tab cycles targets, E picks the nearest, and hold Alt (or the new eye button) to outline everything you can target, as in Diablo.
  • Focus corners (R T F G) keep a character's plate in a corner of the screen. Each corner now shows a pin: click it to pin your target, right-click to clear it, and press H to clear them all. A pinned character who leaves the world now leaves its corner too.
  • Click the icon on any plate to interact with that character.
  • Skill tooltips show real numbers, worked out from your own stats ("12–17 damage"), and skills you can't use right now (no resource, on cooldown, out of range) are darkened on the hotbar.
  • Conversations are a list of spoken replies, with stat checks such as [CHA 12] shown up front.
  • Minions: a strip under your target shows them with their orders (F1–F4).
  • Smaller things: a walk/run toggle (N), a follow key (V), dimmer HUD icons, five HUD buttons a side, an Interact flag, an on-screen keyboard for gamepads, long names that fit their plates, and campaign credits in the System menu.

For campaign creators

  • New entities: Quest and Step (the Quests page), Sheet, Slot and Stat (the Character page), Tree and Talent (talent trees) and Nav (extra pathfinding points).
  • New fields: Actor.auto_attack / auto_attack_interval / auto_attack_range, Actor.on_interact, Actor.icon and Group.icon (shown on plates), Deployment.respawn (monsters come back), Skill.ready and Skill.in_range (hotbar readiness), Condition.all, Menu.hide_unavailable, Resource.combat_text and tags, Sound.volume and range, and Main.credits.
  • New actions: start_auto_attack, stop_auto_attack_self, play_sound_self / play_sound_target, move_home_self, minion commands (follow_master with formations, each_minion, target_master), and echo_* gained a public/private audience, a colour and a duration.
  • Skill descriptions accept {{range: <dice>}} and {{dice: <dice>}}.
  • Test your campaign with scripts: --ui-script plays keys and clicks and checks the screen, with PASS/FAIL lines. See Test your campaign with UI scripts.
  • The validator now rejects a map without a tilemap, a tilemap without a tileset, and an incomplete tileset.
  • Seven new recipes: quest log, talent trees, character page, auto-attack, monsters that come back, skill tooltips and readiness and credits. Every recipe's example is validated against this release.

Multiplayer

  • Tested end to end with real processes: host alone, host with two clients, a dedicated server with two clients, a client quitting and rejoining, and a client crashing mid-fight (the others carry on and the lost player is removed).
  • Fixed: late joiners now see current values; appearance changes reach every player; a change of form no longer leaves ghost bodies or hides the world; discovered waypoints survive a respawn; any-peer messages check who sent them.

Performance

Measured on an Apple M5 with the full Aurelia campaign (about 1,000 actors over three maps), per frame at 60 fps (the budget is 16.7 ms): idle 0.4 ms, walking the moor 2.5 ms, a four-player fight 4.5 ms. On average a host stays under budget with 8 players in one fight (8.7 ms) and with 500 monsters awake at once (14 ms).

Isometry Editor

The editor now matches this release: every new entity and field appears in the inspector, options with a fixed set of words (such as a message's audience) are drop-downs, and Nav points can be placed on the map painter.

v0.401 — Open beta

Isometry is now in open beta. This release covers roughly three months of work bringing the framework to a public, playable state — gameplay, multiplayer, authoring, and a brand-new documentation site.

Gameplay & controls

  • Click to select an actor, click the ground to move, and click again to act — mouse-driven play throughout.
  • Movement keys and the facing keys now update an actor's bearing and heading, so you face the way you move or aim.
  • The cursor reacts to what's under it (move, target, interact).
  • Actor-to-actor interactions now resolve edge-to-edge ("base to base") instead of from centers, so reach feels right.
  • You can move while a dialog or menu is open; talking to someone and then walking away (or clicking empty ground) closes the conversation cleanly.
  • Flying actors: actors can be set to fly over walls and obstacles, stopped only by a new impassable boundary terrain — with fly/land actions and a flying state you can check in your own logic.
  • Null actor: leave a campaign's starting actor unset and players begin as an invisible, immovable spectator — ideal for letting players pick their hero. See Campaign structure.

Camera & map view

  • A pop-out map view that opens from the bottom of the screen with its own zoom, pan (movement/look keys), and recenter-on-player.
  • The world camera remembers your zoom level between sessions.
  • Left-click on the map view centers the camera there.

Skills & actions

  • Click to use skills from the hotbar.
  • A skill's charge time can now affect action dice (e.g. scale damage by how long you held it), and the cast animation holds on its final frame while charging.
  • Action parameters accept dice expressions, and resource minimums/maximums can be dice too — so values can roll at runtime.
  • New particle-effect actions to attach and remove cosmetic effects on actors.
  • Movement tracks accept ordered, numbered waypoints; teleport resumes travel to your original destination after the jump.

Multiplayer

  • Teleports and facing now reach and replicate to the owning client correctly, so what you do shows up the same for everyone.
  • A first-login hook and per-player login statistics.
  • Stability fixes to actors updating correctly on everyone's screen, and joining a game while it's still loading.

Interface

  • Menus (resources, skills, system, and more) now have outlined, keyboard-navigable slots, a dedicated description panel, and a theme-aware selection outline; keybind and gamepad screens are fully keyboard-navigable.
  • Focus plates show an actor's group name in the group's color.
  • The system info screen scrolls with the arrow keys.
  • Reworked the validation-error screen and added syntax highlighting to the error log.
  • Resource icons fit their slots, a placeholder shows for missing icons, and various spacing and theme-opacity polish.
  • Timed-hold keybinds with a configurable hold time.

Campaign authoring

  • Stronger validation: full dice-notation checking and per-action parameter checking, so mistakes surface before the game runs.
  • Actions may omit their function to be animation-only or chain-only.
  • Bad or missing campaign names now fail gracefully instead of crashing.
  • Tiles can carry isolated navigation, and unused optional fields dissolve away on load.

Platform

  • Engine upgraded for performance and stability.
  • Adopted a major.commit-count version scheme.
  • All documentation moved to this website; the player log is written to log.txt.
